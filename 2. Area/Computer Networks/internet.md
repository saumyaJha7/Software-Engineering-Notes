## Q. What is Internet ?
### Ans : The **internet is a global network of computers and devices, physically connected by cables, satellites, and wireless signals**.



# What Happens When You "Switch on the Internet" on Your Phone?

Let’s say you turn on **mobile data** (same idea for Wi-Fi, just a different access point):

---

## 1. Your Phone Connects  
- **Mobile data** → connects to the nearest **cell tower**.  
- **Wi-Fi** → connects to your **home router**, which is wired to your ISP.  

---

## 2. Authentication  
- Your SIM card (for mobile) or router login (for Wi-Fi) identifies you to the **ISP (Internet Service Provider)**.  
- ISP grants access and assigns your phone an **IP address** (like a postal address for your device).  

---

## 3. Requesting Data  
- You open Google/YouTube/etc.  
- Your phone sends a request (like: “Give me www.google.com”).  
- This travels via the cell tower → ISP → submarine cables/fiber → Google’s servers.  

---

## 4. DNS Lookup 🗂️  
- Before reaching Google, your phone asks a **DNS server**: “What is the IP address of www.google.com?”  
- DNS replies with something like **142.250.190.14**.  

---

## 5. Data Travels Back  
- Google’s server sends back the webpage or video as **tiny data packets**.  
- These packets zip across fiber cables, submarine cables, or satellites → to your ISP → to your tower/router → to your phone.  

---

## 6. Phone Reassembles Data  
- Your phone reassembles all the packets.  
- Your browser/app displays them as text, video, or images.  

All of this happens in **milliseconds** ⚡.

---

✅ So:  
- The internet **is physical** (cables, towers, satellites).  
- What you see on your phone are **digital signals carried over that infrastructure**.
